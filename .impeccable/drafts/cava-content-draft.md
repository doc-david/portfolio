## Intro
For my Information Ecosystems class at CU Boulder, I studied how a fast casual restaurant works as an information system. I chose Cava Grill because it's one of my favorite places to eat, and because every bowl is built right in front of the customer, so almost the whole process is visible from the line.

I visited twice: during the Tuesday dinner rush at 6 PM, and again the next afternoon at 3 PM. I ordered and ate my own bowl like any other customer, photographed the space, and sketched a floor map. I wasn't allowed behind the counter, so anything I say about the kitchen is inferred from what I could see from the front.

I analyzed what I saw through distributed cognition, looking at how the work is shared between people (social space), the room and its signs (physical space), and timing (temporal space).

## What I found

### The line does the talking
The assembly line splits the work into fixed steps, so team members barely need to talk. Each one repeats their part, and the only real handoff is when one passes your bowl to the next and mentions any extra charges. **[new]** The bowl itself carries the order down the line: each station can see what's already in it.

### The room gives the directions
The restaurant is laid out in the same order as the meal: order, grab a drink, sit down, and leave through the exit at the back. A permanent barrier shows you where to queue, the big menu covers the chef-curated bowls, and smaller menus on the glass match the toppings in front of you. Even the pickup shelf is split into pickup and delivery, with notes for drivers in English and Spanish.

### The work happens before you arrive
Ingredients are cooked and prepped ahead of time, and the kitchen keeps restocking even when stations are full, so the front can build bowls quickly. The cycle is always the same: prep, build, hand off, clean, repeat.

## Recommendation
The linear layout is efficient, and the signs make it easy for first-timers to follow. The one thing working against it is the large structural pillar in the middle of the room, which blocks a walking path and hides the dining area from the entrance. **[new]** It can't be moved, but it can be put to work: since the pickup signage is already on a pillar, this one could become a landmark that points people to pickup on one side and seating on the other.
